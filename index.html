01 About

Civil engineer turned AI engineer.

I started out running infrastructure projects in Pune, where I spent three years replacing manual reporting with Python and Power BI. That habit of turning tedious work into systems took me to Queen Mary University of London for an MSc in Data Science & AI, which I finished with Distinction.

Since then I've worked on one question: how do you make LLM systems trustworthy enough to run unattended? My answer is usually more engineering, not a bigger model — agents that gather evidence, critics that check it, confidence gates, caches, and a human approval step where it matters.

Today I teach AI to engineering students, coordinate the department's industry outreach, and keep shipping real systems.

Artificial Intelligence — Third & Final Year B.Tech

Shivaji University syllabus · Final Year Sem VII: Artificial Intelligence (PCE-CS704) — 3 h theory + 1 h tutorial a week · Third Year (NEP 2020): Artificial Intelligence (CSE3101) — 3 credits

Unit 1 · 5 lecturesArtificial Intelligence and its issues

Definitions and importance of AI, evolution and applications, classifying AI systems by environment, knowledge-inferring systems and planning, uncertainty and learning systems.

Unit 2 · 6 lecturesProblem solving & heuristic search

Problem solving by search, problem and state spaces, blind search and its types, performance measurement, game playing with mini-max and alpha-beta pruning.

Unit 3 · 7 lecturesProbabilistic reasoning & Markov decision processes

Probability, Bayes' rule, Bayesian networks (representation, construction, inference), temporal models, hidden Markov models; MDPs, utility theory, value and policy iteration, POMDPs.

Unit 4 · 7 lecturesLearning systems & expert systems

Supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning, decision trees; expert-system development stages, probability-based expert systems, tools, difficulties and applications.

Unit 5 · 5 lecturesReinforcement learning

Passive RL, direct utility estimation, adaptive dynamic programming, temporal-difference learning, active RL and Q-learning.

Unit 6 · 6 lecturesAI with Python

NumPy, SciPy, matplotlib, NLTK and SimpleAI; real-time AI applications; case studies on Azure ML, Google AI, Swift AI and TensorFlow.

Students will be able to
  • Evaluate AI methods and describe their foundations.
  • Apply AI principles to problem solving, inference, perception, knowledge representation and learning.
  • Use reasoning and knowledge representation on real-world problems, and explain why search algorithms matter.
  • Build learning and expert systems, and discuss AI's scope, limits and societal impact.
Tutorials & mini-projects (in Python)
  • Uninformed and informed search; game search.
  • Constructing and inferring from Bayesian networks.
  • Value and policy iteration, and reinforcement learning in a grid world.
  • A mini-project — e.g. predicting a user's next location, detecting YouTube comment spam or identifying a song's genre — plus a case study of a real AI application.

Objectives: master the fundamentals of C, understand program flow, build problem-solving logic, and debug and handle errors.

Structure and execution of a C program, character set, tokens, keywords, identifiers, constants, data types, variables; formatted I/O with getchar, putchar, scanf and printf.

Unit 5 · 4 hrsUser-defined functions

Program form, compiling and linking, data types and modifiers, the four C scopes, storage classes, statements; console I/O, multi-dimensional and variable-length arrays.

Unit 2 · 7 lecturesFunctions & pointers

Scope, parameter passing, recursion, prototypes, variable-length parameter lists; pointer operators and expressions, arrays of pointers, multiple indirection, pointers to functions and structures.

Unit 3 · 6 lecturesStructures, unions, enums & typedef

Arrays of structures, passing structures to functions, structure pointers, nested structures, unions, enumerations, sizeof for portability.

Unit 4 · 6 lecturesSearching & sorting

Linear and binary search, hashing and collisions; bubble, selection, insertion, merge, quick and radix sort; complexity analysis.

Unit 5 · 6 lecturesStacks, queues & linked lists

Array-based stacks and queues, circular and priority queues, deques; singly, doubly and circular linked lists.

Unit 6 · 6 lecturesTrees

Binary trees and traversals, binary search trees, AVL trees, B and B+ trees, heaps and heap sort.

Lab work
