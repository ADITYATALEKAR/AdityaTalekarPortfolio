The problem

Banks run thousands of internet-facing endpoints, and they change every week. Most institutions discover an expired certificate, a weak cipher or a forgotten subdomain only during a breach or an audit.

  • Forgotten systems stay exposed long after anyone remembers them.
  • Cryptographic weaknesses fail silently, and quantum-vulnerable keys mean today's traffic can be decrypted later.
  • Hidden trust relationships let one weak system compromise others that look healthy.
  • Scanners give snapshots rather than history, so nobody can say what changed or whether risk is rising.

What I built

From a single seed domain, Guardian does what an attacker's reconnaissance would do, but deterministically and without payloads or login attempts. It discovers the estate, fingerprints every endpoint at protocol level, builds a trust graph, detects weaknesses, predicts which will escalate, and gives analysts a console with an attack simulator.

Discovery (left) feeds an analytical stack (right) that only depends downward. Swap PostgreSQL for the filesystem store with one environment variable.

The six layers

  • Observation. DNS, TCP, TLS and HTTP measurements turned into 24 fingerprints and 10 physical signals such as drift and jitter.
  • Trust graph. Endpoints, identities, certificates and evidence linked by 11 relationship types.
  • Weakness detection. Six detectors fused into clusters such as negotiation disruption.
  • Prediction and memory. Per-endpoint learning state with predictions up to 90 days out.
  • Decision. An eight-phase campaign model (stable, reconnaissance, probe, escalation and so on) with hysteresis so alerts do not flap.
  • Interface. A React console with dashboards, endpoint drill-downs, the trust graph and the simulator.

Engineering decisions

  • Determinism. Same input, same output, always. Even retry jitter comes from a hash, so auditors can replay any finding.
  • Explore, then exploit. Every discovery module gets a 30-second slice; only productive ones get more time, with hard caps on graph size.
  • Observe, never attack. No payloads and no authentication attempts; findings are written in defensive language for a bank's governance review.
  • Small supply chain. Standard-library Python plus three dependencies: less to audit, less to break.
  • Compliance mapping. TLS, certificate, WAF and post-quantum findings mapped to PCI-DSS v4.0, RBI guidance and NIST IR 8413.

Impact

  • One demonstration cycle from a single seed domain found 184 related assets, made 54 of 61 protocol observations, raised 46 TLS findings and flagged 8 as critical, in 6 minutes 43 seconds.
  • Larger estates map 500 or more endpoints in about ten minutes, work that takes a security team hours to days by hand.
  • Presented at IDBI Innovate 2026 as a working product.
  • Business development was automated with an outreach agent that sourced 2,000+ leads and sent up to 200 personalised e-mails a day.

What I learned

  • For a bank, repeatable and auditable beats clever. Determinism became a feature I could sell.
  • Visual metaphors matter: drawing the trust graph as a city made a 900-node graph readable for analysts.
  • Guardian observes only public metadata and is intended for authorised assessment of an organisation's own estate.
