The role

As the owner's engineer on site, I was responsible for getting projects delivered on time and on budget: planning, cost control, billing, people and clients.

What I did

Project delivery

  • Ran day-to-day execution: site coordination, work scheduling, quality checks and progress reporting.
  • Built and maintained schedules in Microsoft Project (work breakdown, dependencies, critical path and baselines) and tracked slippage weekly.
  • Prepared bills of quantities, running-account bills and reconciliation statements in Excel.

Budgeting and cost control

  • Tracked budgeted against actual quantities, rate analysis and material consumption, and explained variances to leadership.
  • Reconciled site measurements against contractor bills before payment, catching over-billing and duplicate claims.
  • Built Excel cash-flow models across overlapping projects so material purchases and labour payments never stalled work.

Clients and stakeholders

  • Point of contact for clients, consultants, sub-contractors and government inspectors, turning technical issues into decisions non-engineers could make.
  • Presented progress and cost reviews to clients and documented change requests so scope creep did not become unpaid work.
  • Used LLMs and web search to speed up plan design and find new clients, saving about two hours of manual work a day.

Team and resources

  • Managed a team of ten (supervisors, surveyors and site staff) with daily task allocation and weekly reviews.
  • Planned labour, machinery and materials across concurrent sites to avoid idle crews and double-booked equipment.
  • Coached junior staff on measurement, documentation and reporting so quality did not depend on one person.

Working under pressure

Reporting that took a month

Situation
Cost, quantity and progress reports were compiled by hand from site registers; by the time they reached the owners they were out of date.
Task
Make reporting fast and reliable enough to drive decisions, without adding staff.
Action
Standardised data capture in Excel templates, wrote Python scripts to consolidate and validate the registers, and built Power BI dashboards on top.
Result
Turnaround fell from about one month to one week, and the owners reviewed live dashboards instead of stale reports.

Keeping sites moving during a cash-flow crunch

Situation
Several projects competed for the same materials, machinery and payments.
Task
Keep every site productive without overspending.
Action
Built a weekly resource and cash-flow plan in Excel and Microsoft Project, re-sequenced non-critical work and negotiated delivery schedules with suppliers.
Result
Avoided idle crews and stop-work days; schedules stayed within the agreed baselines.

Bills that did not match the site

Situation
Contractor bills regularly disagreed with measured quantities.
Task
Protect the company from over-payment while keeping contractor relationships workable.
Action
Introduced measurement before payment with reconciliation sheets and photo evidence, and walked contractors through every discrepancy.
Result
Over-billing was caught before payment, and disputes were settled on data rather than argument.

What I learned

  • Most delays come from information arriving late, not from people working slowly. That is why I started automating reports.
  • Clients trust numbers they can trace. That idea now runs through everything I build with AI.
