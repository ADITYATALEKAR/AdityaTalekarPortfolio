The constraint that shaped everything hereTalekar Infra is a small firm: 6–8 active projects by 2022, a team of about 10 across site, procurement and project management, no enterprise data platform, no BI licence and no AI budget. I was the sole data/AI engineer, alongside project-management responsibilities. Every system below ran on free or open-source tooling — Python, PostgreSQL, Power BI Desktop, Google Colab's free GPU, FastAPI, pgvector, Hugging Face and free-tier LLM APIs. I left in July 2024 to complete a Master's in Data Science and AI at Queen Mary University of London; everything after that sits elsewhere in this portfolio.

Stage 1: Data Engineer / Project Engineer (January 2020 to January 2023)

This phase grew in two steps: a first, basic automation of the monthly Excel consolidation, then a full PostgreSQL and Power BI reporting platform once the firm's project count and data volume grew.

Excel consolidation automation

Business problem. Monthly progress and cost reports were assembled by hand from 2–3 site registers and supplier invoice files kept in Excel. Consolidation took three to four weeks because of inconsistent naming, mixed units and duplicate rows across files, so reports often reached management after decisions had already been made.

Architecture. Python scripts read the Excel site registers, purchase orders and invoice files. Data landed in an early SQLite database, which I migrated to PostgreSQL in 2021 as volume grew. Cleaning covered deduplication, unit normalisation and simple site/material mapping, with null and duplicate checks and basic reconciliation against invoice totals before anything went out. Output was formatted Excel exports, then early Power BI Desktop views, run on a cron/Task Scheduler job before monthly reviews.

Data. 2–3 active projects, about 8 material categories, roughly 10,000 rows processed across registers and invoices, consolidated monthly with ad-hoc checks in between.

Training/evaluation. None — this is data engineering, not a model.

Deployment. Monthly management reviews, using cleaned data instead of hand-consolidated spreadsheets.

Impact. Report turnaround fell from about three to four weeks to about one to two weeks, and manual consolidation time fell from about 20 to about 8 hours a month.

What I'd improve. Move from SQLite to a proper relational database sooner, introduce a site/material master table to stop name drift at the source, and add a simple validation report for management before publishing.

Reporting platform

Business problem. As the firm grew to 6–8 active projects and 28 material categories, the early scripts weren't enough. Reporting needed a curated layer with joins across cost, quantity and progress, reconciled against purchase orders and invoices, with dashboards the team could open themselves instead of waiting for an exported report.

Architecture. Python scripts ingested Excel/CSV exports from site registers, purchase orders, invoices and BOQs into a local staging folder and a raw_* schema in PostgreSQL. Cleaning, deduplication, unit normalisation, site/material mapping and joins fed curated_* tables (projects, sites, materials, purchase_orders, invoices, consumption, progress_updates), with null checks, duplicate checks and reconciliation against PO/invoice totals before publication. Power BI Desktop dashboards and scheduled Excel exports served the result, on a weekly cron/Task Scheduler batch.

Data. 6–8 active projects, 28 material categories, about 72,000 curated rows cumulative over three years, roughly 40–60 rows ingested a day.

Training/evaluation. None — data engineering and reporting automation, not a model.

Deployment. Weekly and monthly project reviews; site and procurement teams checked cost vs. budget, quantity variance and progress against plan directly in Power BI, with reports exported for management.

Impact. Report turnaround fell from about two weeks to about one week, reporting hours from about 25 to about 6 a week, and manual reconciliation from about 12 to about 3 hours a week, while I managed a team of about 10. Taken together with the earlier Excel automation, the cumulative 2020–2023 improvement took monthly reporting from about one month to about one week.

What I'd improve. Incremental ingestion with proper audit columns, a dbt-style transformation layer for clearer lineage, and automated alerts when site entries diverge from PO totals past a threshold.

Stage 2: AI Engineer / Project Engineer (February 2023 to July 2024)

I moved into an AI engineering role in February 2023, shortly after ChatGPT's public launch. My first AI project was a classical ML forecasting model with no LLM involved; production LLM work began after April 2023, once the ChatGPT API was available and I had evaluated it. From there I built a citation-grounded RAG assistant and an LLM-plus-web-search research workflow, all on free-tier APIs and open-source components. As project engineer I also owned scoping, success metrics, evaluation design, stakeholder coordination and handover for this work.

Material demand forecasting

Business problem. Procurement ordered materials reactively, causing emergency purchases and occasional stockouts. With three years of clean consumption data now available from the reporting platform, there was an opportunity to forecast weekly demand by project and material — this was my first AI project, and it used no LLM.

Architecture and data. Trained on about 55,000 site consumption records from 2020–2022 (purchase orders, invoices, project phase, schedule), with lag consumption, rolling averages, project phase, schedule slippage, lead time and seasonality as features. Split by time: trained on 2020–2022, validated on 2023 H1, tested on 2023 H2.

Training and evaluation. Compared a moving-average and naive-last-period baseline against linear regression, XGBoost and Prophet, trained on Google Colab's free GPU and local CPU, evaluated on MAPE, MAE, RMSE and forecast bias.

Deployment. XGBoost was the best performer and ran as a weekly batch job feeding a Power BI/Streamlit dashboard for procurement review, with human override and a reason code recorded for every override.

Impact. MAPE improved from 44% (moving average) to 26% on the held-out 2023 H2 test period, with forecast bias down from +9% to +3%. Emergency purchases fell from roughly 14 to roughly 9 a quarter.

What I'd improve. A separate model per material category instead of one XGBoost model across all of them, better handling for lumpy steel orders (which the model systematically under-forecasts) and better schedule-slippage features.

RAG assistant over project PDFs

Business problem. Specifications, contracts, BOQs, tender documents and project reports were stored as PDFs with no reliable way to search them. Staff needed answers with source citations before using a figure in a project decision — a wrong number pulled from a contract or BOQ has a real cost consequence, so an uncited chatbot answer was not good enough.

Architecture. PDFs were chunked at roughly 450 tokens with 50-token overlap, embedded with a sentence-transformers model and stored in PostgreSQL with pgvector, and the same chunks were indexed in BM25 (rank_bm25) for lexical search. A query ran through both, fused into the top 50 candidates, then a Hugging Face cross-encoder reranked those down to the top 10, behind a FastAPI backend with session memory and no paid vector database. LLM generation was added after April 2023, once the ChatGPT API was available and evaluated — before that the system was retrieval-only. Once added, the LLM answered from only the top 10 chunks, with inline citations to source PDF and page.

Data. About 280 PDFs (specifications, contracts, BOQs, tender documents, project reports), 18–25 pages each, chunked into roughly 15,000 passages.

Training and evaluation. I built a 75-query evaluation set by hand, each with a known correct source chunk, and compared a BM25-only baseline, a dense-only baseline and the hybrid-plus-rerank system on Recall@10, MRR@10 and nDCG@10. I separately audited 60 generated answers for citation groundedness — whether every claim in the answer was supported by its cited chunk.

Deployment. Used internally by project and procurement staff for document search and Q&A before reviewing contracts, BOQs and specifications. Every answer was human-reviewed before it entered a project decision.

Impact. Recall@10 improved from 58% (BM25 baseline) to 84% with hybrid retrieval and reranking; MRR@10 from 0.46 to 0.71; nDCG@10 from 0.51 to 0.76. Citation groundedness was 93% on the 60-answer audit. Document search time fell from roughly 15–20 minutes per query to roughly 2–3 minutes.

What I'd improve. Expand the evaluation set past 75 queries, replace the manual groundedness audit with an automated entailment check, and add a confidence threshold that routes weak retrievals to a human instead of generating an answer.

LLM + web-search research workflow

Business problem. Preliminary plan and design research, and sourcing project leads and clients, took manual hours the firm couldn't staff a research team or lead-generation tool for.

What it was. An LLM-assisted research and drafting workflow, using the OpenAI API (after April 2023) and free-tier LLM APIs, with web search integrated to source leads, clients and reference material, and prompt templates for repeatable output. This is an automation and productivity workflow, not a trained model — there's no accuracy metric to report here, and every output went through 100% human review before any outbound use.

Impact. Saved roughly 2 hours of manual work a day; sourced roughly 40–60 leads a month; drafted roughly 15–20 outreach emails a week, all reviewed by a person before sending.

What I'd improve. Structured lead scoring instead of manual triage, and a CRM integration to close the loop between sourced leads and outcomes.

What I learned

  • The biggest jump in reporting speed came from the first, crude Excel automation — the later platform mattered, but three weeks to under two was the step that changed how management worked.
  • With no budget for paid infrastructure, a manually built evaluation set and a human-review step do more for trust than more compute would.
  • Hybrid retrieval plus reranking beat either BM25 or dense search alone by a wide margin — worth the extra moving part.
  • A single model across every material category is the wrong grain; lumpy materials like steel need their own treatment.
